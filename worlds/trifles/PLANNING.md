# Planning: Trifles

- **Author/year:** Susan Glaspell, 1916
- **Genre:** one-act play
- **Grade band:** 9 to 11
- **Setting:** The Kitchen at the Wright farmhouse on the cold morning the sheriff’s party arrives.
- **Possible roles:** Deputy; County Reporter; Neighbor
- **Speaking and non-speaking characters:** Mrs. Hale and Mrs. Peters speak; County Attorney and Sheriff appear only in narration; Minnie Wright and John Wright do not speak.
- **Shared goal:** Decide what happened in this house and what counts as evidence.
- **Scene 1 artifact candidates:** Mr. Hale’s account; broken jars of preserves; quilt pieces and uneven sewing; birdcage with broken door; box with canary, locked until cage is examined.
- **Scene 1 DOK 3 prompt:** The men call these things trifles. Pick two artifacts. What do they show together that neither shows alone? What does the play suggest about who notices evidence and why?
- **Content notes:** Murder; implied domestic abuse
- **Teacher preview required:** true
- **Source edition:** Project Gutenberg edition of Trifles by Susan Glaspell; verify exact ebook number and edition.

Preview scope: one playable scene only. The full build requires role briefings, passage panel, narration, generated art and audio, artifact questions, and an end screen that retains the Case File.
