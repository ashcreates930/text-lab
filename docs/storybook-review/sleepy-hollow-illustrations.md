## Art style (one style for the whole story)

- One style for every page: a painterly storybook illustration in warm gouache on cream paper, with soft edges and visible brush texture. Gently comic in daylight, shadowy and eerie at night, never gory.
- Palette: autumn golds, russet, and moss green by day; deep blue-black, gray mist, and pale moonlight by night, with lantern amber as the warm accent.
- Setting: a quiet Dutch farming valley near the Hudson River, about 1790: rolling wooded hills, a brook, split-rail fences, a log schoolhouse, stone farmhouses with low sloping roofs, a small church and its wooden bridge.
- Ichabod Crane: very tall and thin, narrow shoulders, long arms and legs, big hands and feet, a small head with large ears, large green glassy eyes, and a long pointed nose; loose, rusty-black clothes. Drawn with affection, like a storybook scarecrow, never mocked cruelly.
- The Headless Horseman: only ever a dark cloaked rider on a huge black horse, seen from a distance or in silhouette, with no head and nothing shown at the neck. Never a wound.
- Never: words, letters, or numbers in the image; blood or injury; caricatured faces; anything a later page reveals.

Consistency plan: Generate Ichabod Crane, the schoolhouse, and the valley once as a reference sheet, then pass that reference with every page so they look the same throughout.

- Pages 1 to 3 are full illustrations. Pages 4 to 6 are "add a missing detail" pictures: each scene leaves out one detail the text names, and the student adds it with a quote. From page 7 on, students illustrate in words.
- Bridge pages (our summaries) never get pictures or frames.
