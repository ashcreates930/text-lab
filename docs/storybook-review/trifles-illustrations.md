## Art style (one style for the whole story)

- One style for every page: a painterly storybook illustration in muted gouache on gray-cream paper, with soft edges, visible brush texture, and a still, quiet stage-like feeling.
- Palette: winter grays, faded blue, washed-out cream, and dark wood, with the stove's dull orange glow as the only warm accent. Cold light from small windows.
- Setting: the kitchen of a lonely farmhouse on a freezing winter morning, about 1916: an iron cookstove, a big kitchen table, a small table, a rocking chair, a cupboard, a sink with pans, a stair door. Only what the stage directions and lines name.
- People: the Sheriff and Mr. Hale are men in middle life; the County Attorney is a young man; all three are bundled up in winter coats. Mrs. Peters is slight and wiry with a thin, nervous face. Mrs. Hale is larger and would usually look comfortable, but looks disturbed.
- Never show: Mrs. Wright or John Wright, anyone upstairs, the body, the rope, the dead bird, or violence. Never words, letters, or numbers in the image, and nothing a later page reveals.

Consistency plan: Generate the kitchen (stove, big table, rocker, cupboard, stair door) and the five people once as a reference sheet, then pass that reference with every page.

- Pages 1 to 3 are full illustrations and page 6 is an "add a missing detail" picture.
- Pages 4 and 5 would have been "add a missing detail" pictures, but they are Mr. Hale's account of finding the body and the rope, so they are writing-only frames (mood and setting, no image is ever generated). The same applies to pages 13, 21, 23, 24, and 29.
- Mrs. Wright never appears on stage, so no picture shows her, even in Mr. Hale's account.
